Yesterday, my wife and I went to the local farmers market to get fresh vegetables for a stirfry. After making that last night and enjoying it immensely, we were left with a lot of vegetables and no idea how to use them (except another stirfry). Today I had a stroke of genius! I would make an Indian dish for a change of pace. What resulted I don’t think can properly be called Indian food, but it was delicious nonetheless!

As a disclaimer: I did not pull this recipe out of my ass (although it may seem like it 😛 ). I based it on a [recipe found on the Food Network website](http://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/aarti-sequeira/chicken-in-creamy-tomato-curry-chicken-tikka-masala-recipe/index.html).[  
](http://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/aarti-sequeira/chicken-in-creamy-tomato-curry-chicken-tikka-masala-recipe/index.html)

The first step is to prep the meat. We had chicken on hand, so chicken it was. After slicing the chicken, I rubbed it with garam masala, ground coriander, ground turmeric, and minced garlic.

[![IMAG0084](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0084-e1373671495600-300x215.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0084-e1373671495600-300x215.jpg)

I let that sit in the fridge for an hour, although it could have been left for longer.

The next step was to prepare the vegetables. I used any and everything we had fresh in the kitchen.

[![IMAG0086](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0086-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0086-300x169.jpg)

From left to right: daikon, onion, carrots, tomato, yukon gold baby potatoes, and bok choy.

After prepping the veggies, it was time to start cooking! To start with, I threw the sliced onion in a pan with ~2 tablespoons of minced garlic, a sprinkling of fennel seeds, and 1 tablespoon of peanut oil and cooked until the onions were soft.

[![IMAG0087](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0087-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0087-300x169.jpg)

Next I mixed in 2 tablespoons of tomato paste, 2 teaspoons of paprika, and a teaspoon of garam masala. I cooked this until the tomato paste covered the onions and the spices were fragerant. This is the point were I got really excited 😀

[![IMAG0089](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0089-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0089-300x169.jpg)

Next I dumped in the rest of the vegetables, excluding the bok choy. I added 2 cups of water and 1-1/2 teaspoons of kosher salt, brought to a boil and simmered, covered, for 20 minutes.

[![IMAG0090](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0090-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0090-300x169.jpg)

While the vegetables were simmering, I stir-fried the chicken in my wok until cooked through.

[![IMAG0091](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0091-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0091-300x169.jpg)

When the veggies were done, I combined everything (including the bok choy) in the wok and brought back to a boil. I let this simmered 10 minutes, leaving the cover off this time to boil off some of the liquid.

[![IMAG0097_BURST001](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0097_BURST001-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0097_BURST001-300x169.jpg)

Finally, to make the sauce a little creamier and richer, I mixed ~2 teaspoons of corn starch in some plain yogurt and added that mixture to the dish.

[![IMAG0098_BURST002](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0098_BURST002-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/experimenting-with-indian-food/IMAG0098_BURST002-300x169.jpg)

And walla! “Indian” food cooked with fresh, local vegetables. We served it with jasmine rice to soak up some of the sauce, and enjoyed it thoroughly!
