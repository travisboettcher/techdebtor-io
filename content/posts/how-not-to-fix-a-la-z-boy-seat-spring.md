Hello you! Yes, you sir, there in the La-Z-Boy recliner! Do you suffer from “my chair is uncomfortable”? If so, you could be one of the tens of people that suffer daily from a broken seat spring! Possible causes of broken seat springs include:

  * jumping on your recliner;
  * letting your children jump on your recliner;
  * piling everyone into your recliner; and
  * piling everyone into your recliner while simultaneously letting your children jump on them.



Well, fear not! With one simple payment of $5, all symptoms of “my chair is uncomfortable” can be cured! Just follow this step-by-step process for fixing La-Z-Boy seat springs, and all your problems will melt away!

# How (Not) to Fix a La-Z-Boy Seat Spring

## Step 1 – Look for Other Projects

As any good procrastinator will tell you, the first thing to do when starting a new project is to find other projects that you been putting off. In my case, I have been meaning to set up my [Ouya ](https://www.ouya.tv/ "Ouya")to steam Google Play Music so I can listen to music through my TV (instead of my phone or laptop). This seemed like the perfect time to tackle this project – after all, I would be working in the living room and would need music!

After doing some research, I discovered you could ‘side-load’ the Google Play store onto the Ouya (the Ouya has it’s own store, which doesn’t include Google Play Music). Although it probably isn’t as hard as it sounds, the number of steps required and the success rate of forum users did not sound promising – remember, I was trying to fix a chair not spend four hours getting music to stream through my TV!

I had another idea – I have XBMC installed on my Ouya! Is it possible there is a Google Play Music plugin for XBMC? As it turns out, [there is](https://github.com/vially/googlemusic-xbmc). After downloading the apk from the [XBMC forums](http://forum.xbmc.org/showthread.php?tid=200640) onto my Ouya (I used a URL shortener to shorten the download link so I didn’t have to pull out a keyboard just to type in the longer URL on the Ouya), I launched XBMC and installed the Add-On. In total, actually getting the add-on took me a couple of minutes; getting it set up and working took me another half-hour or so.

Because I have two-factor authentication turned on ([and you should too](http://lifehacker.com/5938565/heres-everywhere-you-should-enable-two-factor-authentication-right-now)), I needed to set up an application-specific password to access Google Play Music from XBMC. Having done that, I proceed to set up the add-on, entering my username and (application-specific) password. And… it doesn’t work :( I don’t know if the add-in just isn’t working right now with two-factor authentication or what, but I just could not get it to work – I tried three or four times with different generated passwords! As a test, I turned off two-factor authentication and entered my actual password into XBMC. Low and behold, it worked! So I called it good enough, started streaming some Coheed and Cambria (In Keeping Secrets of Silent Earth: 3), and went on to Step 2.

## Step 2 – Eat Lunch

At this point, you’ve probably wasted enough of your day that it would be silly to start repairing the chair before lunch. Grab a bite to eat, watch some Daily Show, and relax – setting up the Ouya and Google Play Music was tiring! After you’re fully rested and nourished, move on to Step 3.

## Step 3 – Figure Out Exactly What is Wrong and Fix it

And by “exactly” I mean “approximately” – you aren’t a professional repairman after all (or maybe you are!). Having worked with La-Z-Boys for a couple of years at a furniture store I knew it was probably a broken seat spring, and although I had never personally fixed one, I was quite confident that it couldn’t be too difficult. Flipping the chair upside down (after removing the back), I discovered what was wrong with my subject:

[![2014-08-02 13.01.55](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.01.55-e1407084171921-150x150.jpg)](http://futurecyb.org/?attachment_id=206) [![2014-08-02 13.01.38](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.01.38-e1407084132954-150x150.jpg)](http://futurecyb.org/?attachment_id=205)

Notice the spring in the middle of both pictures – the one bent up and hitting the footrest mechanism? Yeah, that’s not supposed to bend that way. So we need to replace it.

The first thing to do is to remove the end of the seat spring at the back of the chair:

[![2014-08-02 13.04.05](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.04.05-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.04.05-300x169.jpg)In order to do this, you need to open the clasp thingy (technical term – middle right of the picture) fastening the seat spring to the support wire. I accomplished this with a flat-head screwdriver, loosening up the left and right ‘teeth’ and then the middle one. After the support wire and spring are loose, I was able to remove this half of the broken spring quite easily. Below is a picture of the clasp after I had loosened it up:

[![2014-08-02 13.09.20](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.09.20-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.09.20-300x169.jpg)

 

The part that proved much more difficult was the clasp in the front of the chair – I should have removed the base of the chair (not a hard thing to do with a power drill) to give myself better access, but for some reason did not. After struggling for a bit I did finally get the second clasp free and was able to remove the second half of the broken spring. In doing so, I discovered how exactly I needed to re-fasten the seat spring:

[![2014-08-02 13.15.22](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.15.22-e1407085250683-202x300.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.15.22-e1407085250683-202x300.jpg)This end is held in place by hooking the tooth thingy (again, don’t get overwhelmed by the technical terms) to a board at the base of the seat. To access this board, I had to open the footrest and remove about 20 staples:

[![Remove the staples holding the fabric in place](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.23.25-e1407085540582-150x150.jpg)](http://futurecyb.org/?attachment_id=211) [![Remove more staples](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.23.35-150x150.jpg)](http://futurecyb.org/?attachment_id=212) [![Here's the board we need to use](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.26.33-150x150.jpg)](http://futurecyb.org/?attachment_id=213)

At this point you probably want to have a replacement seat spring. I mean, if you didn’t already know that you would need one you should probably go out and find one now.

Removing the tooth thingy from the end of the broken seat spring, reassemble the front of the chair:

  1. Fit the teeth back into the board;
  2. slip the replacement spring under the support wire and hook it into the tooth thingy;
  3. staple the fabric back into place;
  4. fit the clasp thingy back around the spring and wire; and, finally,
  5. using a pliers, tighten the clasp as best you can.



The next step is crucial: get out your Dremel and attach the metal cutting wheel, because _someone_ messed up and gave you the wrong-sized spring.

[![I would have taken an action shot, but 1\) I feared for my phone; and 2\) I have but two hands.](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.45.54-169x300.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.45.54-169x300.jpg)

I would have taken an action shot, but 1) I feared for my phone; and 2) I have but two hands.

After cutting the spring to the correct length, finishing the project is just a matter of getting the second clasp clasped around the support wire and spring and getting the spring fitted back into place:

[![2014-08-02 13.49.47](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.49.47-300x169.jpg)](/images/archive/futurecyb/how-not-to-fix-a-la-z-boy-seat-spring/2014-08-02-13.49.47-300x169.jpg)

 

And voilà! You are cured of “my chair is uncomfortable”! And all it cost was $5 for a replacement seat spring.

As a side note, the XBMC solution for streaming Google Play Music did not end up working – the add-on is currently too unstable. It kept crashing after playing a couple of songs. So back to that project!
