# Costs

#### JR Pass

The Japan Rail pass was an invaluable tool for getting around Japan. We got ours through https://www.japan-rail-pass.com/ and at the time of this writing is $370 per person for 14 days. Now, don’t let the price deter you – we got the 14 day pass that covered almost all trains in Japan. You can get passes for shorter lengths of stay or for a more concentrated area of Japan; both will make the price more attractive.

With as much as we traveled we really made use of our passes. We rode the length of the main island using shinkansen and were able to use the pass for most local travel as well. The convenience of being able to make a reservation at the train station without having to worry about money was worth it alone.

#### Airplane Tickets

As with any airplane tickets, the price varies by time of year and how soon you are leaving. We bought our tickets about 3 months in advance but traveling during the summer (during Obon season no less) jacked up the price – we spent around $1300 per person. I’ve been keeping an eye on flights during the spring (March) and they have varied from $850 to about $1100 for the cheapest tickets.

We were able to cut down costs by avoiding luggage fees – we both had a carry-on suitcase and backpack, so didn’t need to check any luggage. This also helped while travelling around Japan. If we had larger suitcases travel by train would have been much more difficult. If you do decide to take more luggage, look into takuhaibin. Takuhaibin is like UPS for your luggage and is provided throughout Japan. From what I’ve read most hotels are able to arrange this service for you and you can ship directly to hotels (not as useful if you use something like https://www.airbnb.com/).

# When I Go Back/Suggestions

These are random tips – they are for both your benefit and mine :)

  * If you decide to travel to Hokkaido, give yourself more than a night. Although our trip to Hokkaido was wonderful, it was a lot of travel for just a night’s stay.
  * Definitely go to Miyajima. Definitely leave a whole day for it.
  * Sendai
  * Kumano Nachi Taisha: this is a temple I wasn’t able to make it to my first trip. It would have taken a full day of travel and we hadn’t planned for it, but from the pictures I think that day would be well worth it.
  * Skip Hiroshima Castle unless museums are really exciting for you. We enjoyed Matsumoto Castle so much we decided to go to Hiroshima Castle, but it was primarily a museum to samurai. Although that sounds awesome and to some might be, it wasn’t as cool as Matsumoto Castle.
  * See Mt. Fuji. Somehow we didn’t see one of the most iconic sights of Japan, despite riding a train right by it.
  * Get advance tickets for anything that requires them. We really wanted to visit the Studio Ghibli museum, but found out when we got to Japan that the tickets require about a three month lead time.
  * Use Airbnb (or don’t!). We had several really cool experiences using Airbnb, but unless you are really committed to saving money this is no cheaper than getting hotel rooms. We were also at the mercy of the people we were renting from and that came back to bite us in Kyoto, when we weren’t able get into the house. Everything worked out (we ended up getting a hotel room instead) but it was still an unpleasant experience.
  * Kurobe Gorge
  * Osaka Castle, Nijo Castle
  * Sagano Scenic Line
  * Book any shinkansen tickets you can. For the most part you can’t book tickets outside of Japan, but there are some you can get – the JR-East website has a way to reserve tickets, for example. Any you can’t get outside Japan get as soon as you arrive. If you don’t travel during Golden week or Obon season chances are you won’t need the advance tickets but the peace of mind is quite nice :)
  * Learn “shitei seki” and “jiyu seki”. These two phrases mean “reserved seat” and “unreserved seat”, respectively, and come in handy when lining up at the train station.
  * I accidentally ordered Ordinary JR Passes and saved us $260 and we didn’t miss it, although it did lead to some confusion when we went to reserve our Green class seats!
  * Crazy cult buildings
  * Daibutsu of Ushiku
  * Amanohashidate & Matsushima
