I didn’t realize it had been almost a month since my lost post! In that time, I have been [preparing for a poster presentation](https://www.dropbox.com/s/frvk3s2ydetyqdv/Poster.pdf) for the RenderFarm project (now also known as DSABR – the D is silent). I also went to[ HTCondor Week 2013](http://research.cs.wisc.edu/htcondor/HTCondorWeek2013/) in Madison, Wisconsin and sat in on a day’s worth of talks on Condor!

The trip to HTCondor Week was interesting. I wasn’t really sure what to expect and honestly wasn’t really looking forward to it – it just didn’t sound like my kind of thing. Fortunately, I was wrong! It was fun to push my personal boundaries and meet all these really smart and interesting people. As an example, I ate lunch next to the lead of the HTCondor team, Miron Livny. He initiated the lunch – our group wasn’t being very social – but it was still a good ice breaker. Other contacts I made in Madison were HTCondor team members Becky Gietzel and Nate Panike. They gave us a tour of a server room in the Town Center (part of the Wisconsin Institutes for Discovery) and of a server room in the Computer Sciences building.

Although most of the talks were interesting, there were a few talks that were more captivating than others. For me, the talks that included how Condor was being used to solve real-world problems were the most interesting. Of course, Peter Bui’s presentation on how our group was using HTCondor was the best, but the talks given by Barry Van Veen (Analysis of Electrical Brain Networks with HTCondor) and Leo Singer (HTCondor in MacPorts) were probably second and third in my opinion. The least interesting for me were the talks that felt like they were trying to sell their services to you – they just seemed out of place.

The next update probably won’t be for a while – I’m going to make some improvements to DSABR over summer, but the real work will start again next fall. In the meantime, I will be working on a paper to submit to the IEEE Cluster 2013 conference, and may initiate a collaboration between myself and a mathematics professor on campus in an attempt to apply what I’ve learned in my computer science classes to one of my favorite math subjects, topology.
